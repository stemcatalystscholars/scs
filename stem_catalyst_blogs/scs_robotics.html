Welcome to The Catalyst! - Introduction to Web Development

In this blog post, we introduce The Catalyst, our new blog platform, and share our excitement about exploring STEM concepts. We also provide a brief overview of HTML and CSS, the foundational technologies used to build websites.

Hey! My name is Antara, and I'm one of the co-founders of STEM Catalyst Scholars. For our debut blog post, I just wanted to share how excited we are to launch The Catalyst! However, this is STEM Catalyst Scholars, so of course, we have to talk about STEM. First of all, this website was designed and coded by our own team! Using HTML and CSS, we built the website you're exploring right now. But you might be wondering: what even is HTML? HTML stands for HyperText Markup Language. It's the language used to create the structure of a website. Think of it as the framework of a building: it organizes the content into headings, paragraphs, images, links, and more. Honestly, HTML is a great starting point for learning how to code. Its basic syntax is relatively straightforward to understand, and once you get comfortable with the fundamentals, there's so much you can create! HTML, however, mainly focuses on a website's structure and content. Without styling, a webpage can look pretty plain. Imagine a page with nothing but basic headings, paragraphs, and black text. Pretty boring, right? That's where CSS comes in! CSS stands for Cascading Style Sheets, and it's what we use to style websites. It controls things like colors, fonts, spacing, layouts, and even animations. When building a website, you can use HTML classes to group elements together and apply specific styles to them through CSS. For example, this very blog page uses different classes to style different sections, from the navigation bar at the top to the title and the article you're reading right now. By combining HTML and CSS, we were able to turn a basic webpage into the website we envisioned for STEM Catalyst Scholars! And the best part? You don't need to be an expert programmer to start experimenting with these tools. With a little curiosity, some patience, and plenty of trial and error, you can begin creating things of your own. This is just the beginning of The Catalyst. We're excited to use this blog to explore STEM concepts, share ideas, and show how science, technology, engineering, and mathematics connect to the world around us. Who knows? Maybe this post will inspire you to build your very first website! Thanks for being part of our journey. We can't wait to see what we'll discover together.
